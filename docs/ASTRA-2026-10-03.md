# Astra x Haven — First collaboration

**Date:** October 3, 2026  
**Project:** Haven  
**Collaborator:** Astra  
**Status:** First completed design/build pass together

## Why this matters

This is the first thing built collaboratively with Astra for Haven.

The work clarified Haven away from a dense process-monitor / life-OS metaphor and toward a much simpler product idea:

> **One mind → one flow → one screen.**

ChatGPT is where the thinking happens. Haven is the quiet visual instrument that resolves that thinking into a small amount of useful state.

The product should be understood in roughly three seconds.

## Direction given to Astra

The brief emphasized:

- Haven is not a task manager, second brain, project manager, or dashboard suite.
- No folders, workspaces, nested pages, or separate work/personal/creative modes.
- No process-table telemetry, PID/state metadata, charts, cards, or configuration panels.
- The core interaction is **glance → know → act**.
- AI should be backstage. The screen itself should stay quiet and deterministic-looking.
- Haven should help the user orient, act, and then leave the screen.
- A major philosophical reference is Wendell Berry: technology should remain a tool inside a larger human life rooted in place, people, work, attention, land, craft, faith, music, and physical reality.

Working rule:

> **Work on the screen. Live off the screen.**

## Visual language

Astra was asked to preserve and refine the emerging **Cole Calfee / 1994 personal workstation** language:

- warm gray / mushroom field
- monochrome black type
- Monaco / monospace
- hard 1–2 px rules
- square checkboxes
- centered composition
- one viewport when possible
- no rounded corners
- no shadows
- no gradients
- no glass
- no bright tech colors
- no generic SaaS visual language
- editorial restraint
- early Macintosh / architecture-studio workstation energy
- private, useful, human — not cyberpunk

Branding should remain minimal. A small label such as **PERSONAL / 1994** is enough.

## Preferred Astra result

Astra produced a finished local HTML screen and later published a separate private ChatGPT Site version.

The preferred visual result used quiet blocks rather than a dense data table. The composition included:

- **NOW PLAYING**
- **START HERE**
- **TO DO**
- **OUTREACH**
- **READING**
- **MONEY**
- **ACTIVE THREADS**
- **DIRECTION**
- a tiny footer / system note

A particularly successful detail was pairing the current music with a small instruction such as **SET THE TONE.**

The interface felt more like a daily instrument than a dashboard.

## Content architecture

Haven should surface the right things, not everything equally.

Current canonical categories:

### Priorities
What deserves attention now.

### To do
Concrete next actions.

### Outreach
People to contact or follow up with.

### Reading
A small active reading stack that supports the current season.

### Money

## Product principle after the Astra pass

The main lesson from this first collaboration:

> **The intelligence belongs in selection, not in visible complexity.**

Haven should become smarter by deciding what deserves the foreground, not by exposing more metadata or asking the user to manage more state.

The success test remains:

> **Can Cole open this, understand his life in three seconds, and then get back to living it?**
