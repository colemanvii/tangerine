01 / THE IDEA

A home should know itself.

A new home contains an extraordinary amount of knowledge while it is being built: drawings, field photographs, materials, equipment, finishes, warranties, contractors, service requirements, decisions, and details that will eventually disappear behind walls.

At turnover, much of that knowledge becomes fragmented across binders, inboxes, apps, contractors and people's memories.

Haven gives the house a permanent memory.

02 / HAVEN

A calm operating system for the home.

Instead of asking a homeowner to understand twenty different systems, Haven interprets the house first.

03 / FREEMAN

The record begins before move-in.

Freeman has an unusual opportunity because Haven can begin during construction rather than after the homeowner receives the keys.

PLANSWhat was designed.

Drawings, engineering, revisions and details.

FIELDWhat was actually built.

Progress photography and behind-the-wall records.

MATERIALSWhat the house is made of.

Paint, stone, flooring, cabinetry, fixtures and hardware.

SYSTEMSWhat keeps it running.

HVAC, electrical, plumbing, appliances and network.

PEOPLEWho knows the work.

Builder, architect, designer and service contacts.

OWNERSHIPWhat happens next.

Manuals, warranties, maintenance and service history.

06 / WHY

Extend the quality of the build beyond turnover.

This does not require Freeman to become a technology company. It means preserving the intelligence already created through the act of building well.

For Freeman, it creates a durable connection between the builder and the work long after construction ends.

Built for Generations becomes something the owner can continue to experience.

07 / FIRST TEST

One house.

Don't launch a platform. Don't sell a technology package. Don't overbuild it.
