# Haven Home — Product Direction

## Thesis

The modern home has dozens of systems and almost no coherent memory. Haven is the calm layer above them.

It is not another smart-home remote. It is an orientation system: a durable, legible view of the house and household that reduces uncertainty and preserves continuity.

## Four questions

Every primary Haven surface should answer, in order:

1. What is happening?
2. Are we okay?
3. What needs me?
4. What happens next?

If a module does not help answer one of those questions, it probably does not belong on the primary surface.

## Information architecture

### Home
A brief, not a dashboard. Overall state, exceptions, one or two attention items, and the next relevant events.

### Systems
Power, climate, water, network, appliances, generator, irrigation, environmental sensors. Prefer plain-language state over charts.

### Security
Doors, gates, access, perimeter and meaningful exceptions. Haven is an interface layer, not a substitute for professionally designed life-safety or security systems.

### Household
Residents, guests, arrivals, departures, routines, shared notes and household context. Privacy must be explicit and controllable.

### Record
The permanent memory of the house:
- plans and specifications
- finish selections and paint codes
- photographs of concealed conditions
- equipment models and serials
- warranties and manuals
- service history
- contractor and builder records
- maintenance intervals
- ownership handoff notes

## Builder-to-homeowner continuity

The record can begin before the homeowner moves in.

During construction:
PROJECT → FIELD → DOCUMENTS → TEAM

After turnover:
HOME → SYSTEMS → SECURITY → HOUSEHOLD → RECORD

The underlying idea is continuous: the system knows the place, preserves decisions, and surfaces only what matters.

## Interaction principles

- Quiet is a feature.
- Exceptions outrank telemetry.
- No notification should exist merely because data exists.
- Green = normal / safe state.
- Signal orange = attention or decision.
- Red is reserved for genuine urgent states and must never be decorative.
- Machine state uses monospace.
- Human meaning uses editorial typography.
- Controls should feel deliberate, tactile and scarce.
- Avoid gamification, feeds, engagement loops and ambient anxiety.
- Never imply a safety guarantee from incomplete sensor data.

## Visual world

Warm ivory, charcoal, deep instrument green, signal orange, restrained utility color.

References are conceptual rather than imitative: aviation-grade legibility, automotive tactile calm, field instruments, durable American utility, printed manuals, and architectural material warmth.

The terminal is a component, not the identity.

## Prototype roadmap

**01 — Daily Brief**
Prove the core orientation loop with static representative data.

**02 — Home Record**
Model a real house record: room, system, component, document, service event, decision.

**03 — Builder Handoff**
Demonstrate how construction documentation becomes homeowner memory.

**04 — Live Adapter**
Connect one low-risk read-only source and prove that real data can remain calm.

**05 — Wall Surface**
Test the system on a dedicated tablet-sized display with distance legibility, touch targets and idle state.

## Test

A successful Haven prototype should make someone say:

> I understand my house in ten seconds.

Not:

> Look how much data this dashboard has.
