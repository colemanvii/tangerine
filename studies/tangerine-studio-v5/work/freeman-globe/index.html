Motion study 001

The script holds perfectly still. Behind it, the world turns.

01

One idea.

The signature does not move. Only the globe turns, on its vertical axis, at a constant rate.

It should read as a flat printed insignia from another century that happens to be rotating. Two colours, Freeman ivory and Freeman red. No type, no texture, no shadow, no grain.

02

A true bitmap.

The mark is drawn on its own 160-pixel grid, and every frame is one bit: a pixel is red, or it is paper.

In the film, each of those pixels is exactly four by four video pixels, so the grid stays even and the edges stay hard. Around the script, the globe is cleared by one pixel so the signature always reads.

03

Slow.

The first round turned once every 13.09 seconds, the pace of Freeman’s own globe study. It was slowed for the second round at Cole’s request: one revolution now takes sixteen.

Because the globe repeats every twenty degrees, any multiple of twenty closes the loop exactly. An eight-second film turns it 180°.

04

The pendant.

A second film of the same mark: the whole insignia, script and globe together, turning as one object, like a pendant on a chain.

The script becomes a flat plate through the axis, one bitmap pixel thick. Face-on, it is the mark. Edge-on, the whole signature is a single vertical line, as heavy as a line of the globe. Past that it reopens, mirrored: the back of the pendant.

Face-on, it is the mark. Edge-on, it is a line.

05

Nothing was redrawn.

The films read Freeman’s original code and data and paint what they produce. The script inside the globe is checked, pixel for pixel, against the production Freeman bitmap. Freeman’s own repository and websites were not touched.

The globe on this page works the same way: it is drawn live, in your browser, from a byte-for-byte copy of the original mark file.
