## What Tangerine actually is

Tangerine is the independent creative world and studio of Cole Calfee: art, design, photography, writing, film, creative direction, and collaborations with ambitious founders. It is **not** a consumer productivity app, a typical advertising agency, or a software dashboard. Older Haven/Tangerine product experiments in this repository are historical and should not be treated as the current business.

The guiding principle is **“In cyberspace as it is in heaven.”** Translate that into beauty, order, warmth, humanity, craft, and delight. Do not use it as a decorative substitute for design quality.

## What must survive from V1

- The oversized, warm editorial **tangerine** identity and distinctive orange signature.
- Off-white paper, deep ink, expansive negative space, sophisticated display typography.
- Independent authorship and an artist's personal universe rather than generic agency marketing.
- Editorial sections: opening, selected work (including Freeman and Joseph Martin), journal, philosophy/about, contact.
- The **small, pixel-crisp blinking tangerine**. Treat it like a signature/cursor: extremely restrained and occasional, not an attention-grabbing loop. Preserve reduced-motion accessibility.
- The first pass's warmth, simplicity, and confidence.

## What to raise substantially

1. **Art direction and editorial hierarchy.** Establish a page rhythm that feels like a remarkable printed art-and-culture publication translated to the web. Make each section compositionally distinct without excessive decoration.
2. **Project storytelling.** Take Freeman and Joseph Martin beyond generic portfolio tiles. Develop compelling editorial approaches to subject, material, people, process, and outcome. Use *verified* project facts and assets only. Current V1 art is conceptual; do not portray it as real completed-project photography.
3. **Typography.** Refine the scale, spacing, ligatures, line breaks, label system, captions, and mobile rhythm. Be rigorously intentional.
4. **Originality.** Think of an author-led studio with a point of view. Strong visual systems are welcome; portfolio clichés are not. No rounded cards, gradients, lazy hover effects, generic stock, or fake case-study text.
5. **Journal.** Make this a credible, inviting space for Cole's original essays, field notes, photographs, and observations. Do not invent personal experiences or imply fictional journal entries are real.
6. **Motion and interaction.** The fruit blink is the motif. Any additional animation should be sparse, purposeful, performant, and respectful of `prefers-reduced-motion`.
7. **Responsiveness and quality.** Make the 390px mobile experience every bit as considered as desktop. Test 375/390/768/1440 widths, keyboard navigation, contrast, layout overflow, load behavior, and image handling.

## How to work

- Begin with a brief **PRESERVE / IMPROVE** critique of the actual V1 source. Identify the five highest-leverage upgrades.
- Then **build** the V2 HTML/CSS/JS directly, with meaningful refinements and one or two exceptional editorial details rather than dozens of gimmicks.
- Use real artwork/photography when present and properly authorized; if missing, retain or improve intentional illustrative studies and clearly label incomplete content. Do not let missing client assets stop an otherwise complete design iteration.
- Keep V1 intact, commit V2 in its own study directory, and leave the original working study reviewable.
- Render desktop/mobile screenshots and examine them yourself; fix any errors or ugly compositions before presenting.
- End with links to the branch, relevant commits, pull request, preview if available, and concise notes on what remains for final publication.

## Success criterion

When the homepage opens, it should feel unmistakably **Tangerine**: authored, curious, cultivated, warm, and unusually well made. It should make someone want to explore the work and know the person behind it.

Do not aim for an ordinary "nice agency website." Make something worth keeping for ten years.
